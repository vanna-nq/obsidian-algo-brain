# PAPER-072 — On the Impossibility of Informationally Efficient Markets

**Authors:** Sanford J. Grossman; Joseph E. Stiglitz  
**Publication:** American Economic Review 70(3), 393–408 (1980)  
**Primary research source:** https://www.jstor.org/stable/1805228

> **Evidence classification: peer-reviewed publication or conference source record and abstract reviewed, not a full-text audit.** These are summaries of the authors' claims and contributions, not strategy endorsements. No new executable strategy, live results or independently verified historical returns.

## Mechanism / mathematical idea
Formalizes why costly information acquisition and perfect informational efficiency cannot generally coexist in equilibrium: informed traders require incentives.

## Author-reported finding or theoretical scope
Foundational theoretical framework explaining possible rewards to information acquisition, not proof of persistent exploitable alpha.

## Required data / feasibility gate
Explicit information acquisition costs, observable signals, forecast calibration, friction-aware trading policy and equilibrium assumptions.

## Critique / potential failure
Market data may embody competing information and fast reaction; measured predictive information can be too small to overcome acquisition and trading costs.

## Falsifiable follow-up (proposal only)
Evaluate net marginal utility from adding one new costly data source over a price-only baseline, subtracting all access and execution costs.
