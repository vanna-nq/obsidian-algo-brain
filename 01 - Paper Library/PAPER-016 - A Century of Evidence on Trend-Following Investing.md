# PAPER-016 — A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing

**Authors:** Brian Hurst; Yao Hua Ooi; Lasse Heje Pedersen  
**Publication:** The Journal of Portfolio Management 44(1), 15–29 (2017)  
**Verified source:** https://doi.org/10.3905/jpm.2017.44.1.015  
**Evidence level:** Publication metadata and publisher / author abstract or accessible summary screened. Full-text derivations, exact formulas and study tables have **NOT** been independently audited here.

## Core contribution / mathematical mechanism
Extends multi-asset trend following evidence using historical global market data back to 1880.

## Source-supported result or scope
Authors report positive average time-series momentum performance in each decade of the sample and positive performance in 8 of 10 major historical 60/40 drawdowns.

## Risks and limitations
Long-run simulated series have variable market accessibility, histories, cost assumptions and reconstruction uncertainty.

## Required data for replication
Historical futures/proxy mappings, contract rolls, funding, currency returns, scaling methods and source reliability.

## Falsifiable follow-up experiment (proposed only)
Independently examine post-2017 real tradable futures net outcomes and trend performance in contrasting equity/bond crisis regimes.
