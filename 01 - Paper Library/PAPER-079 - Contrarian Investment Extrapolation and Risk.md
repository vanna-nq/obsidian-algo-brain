# PAPER-079 — Contrarian Investment, Extrapolation, and Risk

**Authors:** Josef Lakonishok; Andrei Shleifer; Robert W. Vishny  
**Publication:** The Journal of Finance 49(5), 1541–1578 (1994)  
**Primary research source:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1994.tb04772.x
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1994.tb04772.x

> **Evidence classification: peer-reviewed publication or conference source record and abstract reviewed, not a full-text audit.** These are summaries of the authors' claims and contributions, not strategy endorsements. No new executable strategy, live results or independently verified historical returns.

## Mechanism / mathematical idea
Examines value/contrarian equity selection and whether prices extrapolate poor versus strong fundamentals excessively.

## Author-reported finding or theoretical scope
Authors report historical long-horizon value strategy outperformance and argue investor extrapolation, rather than risk alone, contributes.

## Required data / feasibility gate
Point-in-time sales/earnings/book ratios, stock universe, delisted/failed firms, borrow costs, realized fundamentals and multi-year holding periods.

## Critique / potential failure
The paper concerns fundamental value vs glamour sorting over long horizons, NOT intraday reversion; microcap liquidity and distressed-stock risk can distort results.

## Falsifiable follow-up (proposal only)
Compare value-vs-glamour sorts and risk exposures in post-1994 point-in-time samples across fixed horizon and liquidity strata.
